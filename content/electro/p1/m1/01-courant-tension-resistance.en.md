An electric circuit is a **closed loop**: a source (cell, battery, power supply) pushes electric charges through wires and components, then takes them back. If the loop is open, because of a switch or a broken wire, nothing flows any more.

![9 V cell, closed switch and lit lamp, with the arrow of current I](/static/content/electro/en/m1/circuit-simple.svg)

## Three quantities not to confuse

- **Voltage U**, in volts (V), is the electrical "pressure" between two points. A 9 V cell keeps 9 V between its terminals. A voltage is always measured **between two points**.
- **Current I**, in amperes (A), is the flow of charges through a wire. In electronics we mostly talk about milliamperes: 1 mA = 0.001 A.
- **Resistance R**, in ohms (Ω), tells how strongly a component opposes the current. 1 kΩ = 1,000 Ω and 1 MΩ = 1,000,000 Ω.

By convention, current leaves the source through its **+** terminal, goes round the circuit and comes back through the **−** terminal. This "conventional" direction is the one used by every arrow in this course.

## Measuring without mistakes

To measure a current, you must **open the circuit** and insert the ammeter: it is connected **in series**. To measure a voltage, you simply place the voltmeter probes across the component: it is connected **in parallel**.

![Ammeter in series in the loop and voltmeter in parallel across the resistor](/static/content/electro/en/m1/mesures.svg)

Connecting an ammeter in parallel creates a short circuit: at best the meter's fuse blows, at worst the battery heats up. Before each measurement, check the range and where the leads are plugged in.

## Key points

- Voltage in volts (between two points), current in amperes (in a wire), resistance in ohms.
- Ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel.
- An open circuit lets no current through.
