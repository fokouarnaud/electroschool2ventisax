This mini-project is done in the workshop, with your trainer. Bring your completed measurement table.

## Goal

Calculate an LED's series resistor, check the resulting current and observe the effect of connecting it the wrong way round.

## Parts

| Quantity | Component |
|---|---|
| 1 | breadboard and wires |
| 1 | 9 V battery and its clip |
| 2 | LEDs: one red, one green (5 mm) |
| 3 | resistors: 330 Ω, 470 Ω, 1 kΩ |
| 1 | 1N4148 or 1N4007 diode |
| 1 | multimeter |

## Steps

1. Identify each LED's anode (longer leg) and the diode's cathode (band).
2. Calculate R for a red LED at 20 mA from 9 V, then pick the nearest available value above it.
3. Build battery, resistor and red LED. Measure the voltage across the LED and the current.
4. Replace the red LED with the green one, keeping the same resistor. What happens to the voltage and the current?
5. Add the diode in series. Measure its voltage. Turn it round: what happens?

## Safety

Never connect an LED directly to the battery without a resistor.
