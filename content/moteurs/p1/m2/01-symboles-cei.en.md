An electrical diagram is a language: each device is shown by a **standard symbol**, the same in every country applying the international standard **IEC 60617**. Being able to read them means being able to troubleshoot a cabinet you have never seen.

## Control circuit symbols

The control circuit carries orders (start, stop) at low power, often at 24 V.

![Control symbols: make contact, break contact, start button, stop button, coil, indicator](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/symboles-commande.svg)

- The **make contact** (also called "normally open") is open at rest and closes when the device is operated.
- The **break contact** ("normally closed") is closed at rest and opens when the device is operated.
- A **push button** is a contact fitted with an actuator: the dotted line ending in a T shows the push of the hand.
- The **coil** (rectangle) drives a contactor or a relay; the **indicator** (crossed circle) shows a state.

All contacts are drawn **at rest**: device not operated, coil not energised.

## Power circuit symbols

The power circuit carries the energy from the supply to the motor.

![Power symbols: fuse, circuit breaker, isolator, contactor contact, overload relay, three-phase motor](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/symboles-puissance.svg)

Power contacts look like control contacts; a small sign on the fixed contact tells which device it is: a cross for the **circuit breaker**, a bar for the **isolator**, a half-circle for the **contactor**. The **overload relay** is a rectangle crossed by a zigzag, the **motor** a circle marked "M" and "3~" for three-phase.

## Key points

- IEC 60617: the same symbols everywhere.
- Contacts are always drawn at rest.
- The sign on the fixed contact tells breaker, isolator and contactor apart.
