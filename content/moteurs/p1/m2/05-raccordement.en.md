The three windings of a three-phase motor can be connected in two ways, with **links** fitted on the terminal plate.

## Star connection (Y)

A single horizontal link joins the three outputs W2, U2, V2: they form the common point of the star. The supply arrives on U1, V1, W1.

![Star connection: one link joins W2, U2 and V2; on a 400 V supply, each winding gets 230 V](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/etoile.svg)

Each winding then sits between a phase and the common point: it gets the **phase voltage**, U / √3. On a 400 V line-to-line supply: 400 / √3 = **230 V** per winding.

## Delta connection (Δ)

Three vertical links join U1 to W2, V1 to U2, W1 to V2: each winding sits directly between two phases.

![Delta connection: three vertical links; each winding gets the line-to-line voltage](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/triangle.svg)

Each winding then gets the full **line voltage**. On a 230 V line-to-line supply, each winding gets **230 V**.

## Reversing the direction of rotation

To reverse the direction of a three-phase motor, **swap two phases** at the input (for example L1 and L2). The connection itself does not change. It is always done with the motor locked out.

## Key points

- Star: one link, each winding gets U / √3.
- Delta: three links, each winding gets U.
- Two phases swapped: the motor turns the other way.
