The circuit diagram is used to understand and troubleshoot. Other diagrams meet other needs.

## The single-line diagram

![Single-line diagram of a motor feeder: one line crossed by three small strokes represents the three phases](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/unifilaire.svg)

The three phases are shown by **a single line**, crossed by as many small oblique strokes as there are conductors. It shows at a glance the architecture of a distribution: boards, feeders, protection. It is used for installation drawings and studies.

## The block diagram

![Block diagram: supply, protection, control, motor, machine](/static/content/moteurs/en/m2/synoptique.svg)

Simpler still, the **block diagram** lines up blocks linked by arrows. It says nothing about the wiring, but explains **what each part does**. It is the diagram you show a customer or a newcomer.

## The wiring diagram

The **wiring diagram** draws the devices at their real place in the cabinet, with every wire from one terminal to another and its wire marking. It guides the wireman, but it is hard to read to understand how something works: for that, go back to the circuit diagram.

| Diagram | Used to |
|---|---|
| block | explain the role of each part |
| single-line | show the architecture of a distribution |
| circuit | understand and troubleshoot |
| wiring | wire the cabinet wire by wire |

## Key points

- Single-line: one line for three phases.
- Block diagram: blocks and arrows, no wiring.
- Circuit diagram to understand, wiring diagram to wire.
