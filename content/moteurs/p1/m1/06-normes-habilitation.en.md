Safety rules are not improvised: they come from **standards** and from each country's **regulations**.

## Voltage ranges

Installations are classified by voltage range, because the risks and the rules change with it.

![AC voltage ranges: ELV up to 50 V, LV up to 1,000 V, HV-A up to 50 kV, HV-B above](/static/content/moteurs/en/m1/domaines.svg)

- **ELV** (extra-low voltage): up to 50 V AC, 120 V DC. The 24 V bench of this course belongs here.
- **LV** (low voltage): up to 1,000 V AC, 1,500 V DC. Industrial 400 V motors belong here.
- **HV-A** then **HV-B**: distribution and transmission networks.

## International standards

The **International Electrotechnical Commission** (IEC) publishes standards adopted by most countries, which take them over under their own national reference.

![Some useful IEC standards: IEC 60617 symbols, IEC 60479 effects of current, IEC 60364 installations, IEC 60947 switchgear, IEC 60204-1 machines](/static/content/moteurs/en/m1/normes.svg)

- **IEC 60617**: graphical symbols for diagrams (module 2).
- **IEC 60479**: effects of current on the human body (section 1).
- **IEC 60364**: low-voltage electrical installations (earthing systems, protection).
- **IEC 60947**: low-voltage switchgear (contactors, circuit breakers, relays).
- **IEC 60204-1**: electrical equipment of machines (emergency stop, button colours).

## Authorisation

Working on or near an electrical installation requires an **authorisation**: the employer recognises, in writing, that a trained person can safely carry out a given type of work (non-electrical operations, work, lockout, troubleshooting). An authorisation is not a diploma: it depends on the job, is renewed after refresher training and can be withdrawn. Its terms are set by each country's regulations: ask your employer.

## Key points

- ELV ≤ 50 V AC, LV ≤ 1,000 V AC.
- IEC standards set symbols, protection and switchgear.
- The authorisation is issued by the employer, for a specific job.
