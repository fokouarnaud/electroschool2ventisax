Before working on a motor, a cabinet or a machine, it is **locked out**: it is de-energised safely and lastingly, so that nobody can re-energise it during the work. Lockout always follows the same order.

![The five lockout steps: isolate, lock, identify, verify the absence of voltage, earth and short-circuit](/static/content/moteurs/en/m1/etapes.svg)

1. **Isolate** the equipment from every energy source, with an **isolating** device whose opening is visible or reliable (isolator, switch-disconnector). A plain stop button does not isolate: it controls.
2. **Lock** the device in the open position: personal padlock, and a **tag** stating who is working and why. Each worker fits their own padlock.
3. **Identify** the equipment you are about to work on, to be sure it is the one that has just been isolated.
4. **Verify the absence of voltage** on site, between all conductors and between each one and earth.
5. **Earth and short-circuit** the conductors when there is a risk of voltage returning (lines, capacitors, multiple sources).

## Verifying the absence of voltage

Use a **voltage tester** designed for this, not a multimeter set at random. The tester itself could be faulty, so it is tested before and after.

![Verifying the absence of voltage: try the tester on a known source, measure on the equipment, try the tester again on the known source](/static/content/moteurs/en/m1/vat.svg)

If the tester no longer works at the last check, the measurement in the middle proves nothing: start again with another device.

Removing the lockout follows the reverse order: remove the earthing, check that everybody is out, owners remove their padlocks, then re-energise.

## Key points

- Isolate, lock, identify, verify, earth.
- One padlock and one tag per worker.
- Tester checked before and after the verification.
