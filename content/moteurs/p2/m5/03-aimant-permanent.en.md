In the **permanent-magnet** motor, the field winding is replaced by magnets. It has only **two terminals**, those of the armature.

![Symbol of the permanent-magnet DC motor: two terminals, + and −, to the armature](/static/content/moteurs/en/m5/ap-symbole.svg)

It is the motor of low powers: windscreen wipers, car fans, cordless drills, toys, small pumps, at 6 to 48 V. It is simple, compact and uses no energy to create its field.

## Its characteristic

At constant voltage, its speed **falls slightly, in a straight line**, when the torque demand rises. And its speed is roughly **proportional to the applied voltage**.

![Speed versus torque at 12 V and 6 V: two parallel lines, speed falls slightly as torque rises](/static/content/moteurs/en/m5/caracteristique.svg)

**Example.** A motor turns at 3,000 rpm no-load at 12 V. At 6 V, it turns at about 1,500 rpm no-load. That is how its speed is set: by setting its voltage (section 6).

Its current follows the **torque**: the heavier the load, the more current the motor draws. A stalled motor draws its maximum current and heats quickly.

## Key points

- Permanent magnets: two terminals, no energy for the field.
- Speed roughly proportional to voltage.
- Current proportional to torque: a stalled motor heats up.
