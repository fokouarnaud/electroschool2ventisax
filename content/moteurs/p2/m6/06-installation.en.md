A good motor badly installed soon fails. Installation is done with the motor **locked out** (module 1).

## Mounting and alignment

The motor is fixed on a flat, rigid base, by its feet (**B3** mounting) or by a flange (**B5** mounting). When it drives the machine through a **coupling**, the two shafts must be exactly in line.

![Coupling alignment: shafts aligned, parallel offset, angular misalignment](/static/content/moteurs/en/m6/alignement.svg)

A **parallel offset** or an **angular misalignment**, even of a few tenths of a millimetre, loads the bearings, makes the assembly vibrate and wears the coupling. Alignment is checked with a straightedge, a dial gauge or a laser. With a **belt** drive, the tension must be right: too tight, it wears the bearings; too slack, it slips.

## Connection

![Terminal box: cable through a cable gland, links according to the connection, PE conductor on the earth terminal](/static/content/moteurs/en/m6/raccordement.svg)

1. The cable enters through a tight **cable gland**, which ensures sealing (the motor's IP).
2. The **links** are fitted according to the connection read on the plate (star on a 400 V supply for a 230/400 V motor).
3. The **protective conductor PE** is tightened on the terminal box earth terminal, always.
4. The **conductor size** is chosen according to current, length and installation method (module 9); too low a voltage at the terminals makes the motor heat.
5. The protection (motor circuit breaker or overload relay) is set to the **nameplate current**.

Before commissioning, the **direction of rotation** is checked with a brief pulse, machine uncoupled if needed.

## Key points

- Rigid base, shafts aligned.
- PE always connected; links according to the plate.
- Protection set to the nameplate current; direction checked before commissioning.
