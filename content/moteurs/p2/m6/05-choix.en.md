Choosing a motor means reading its nameplate (module 2) and checking that it matches what the machine needs.

## Power, speed and torque

The output power P and the speed n give the **output torque**:

**T = P / ω**   with   **ω = 2π × n / 60**   (T in N·m, P in W, n in rpm)

**Example.** A 2.2 kW motor at 1,440 rpm: ω = 2π × 1,440 / 60 ≈ 150.8 rad/s, T = 2,200 / 150.8 ≈ **14.6 N·m**. For the same power, a slower motor delivers more torque: a 2.2 kW at 960 rpm gives about 21.9 N·m.

## Efficiency and IE classes

Efficiency is classified by IEC 60034-30-1: **IE1** (standard), **IE2** (high efficiency), **IE3** (premium), **IE4** (super premium). An IE3 motor costs a little more but, on a motor running thousands of hours a year, the energy saving quickly pays back the difference.

## Insulation class and temperature

The insulation class sets the maximum temperature the windings can withstand.

![Insulation classes: 130 °C in class B, 155 °C in class F, 180 °C in class H](/static/content/moteurs/en/m6/classes.svg)

Common motors are **class F**. Insulation working 10 °C above its limit has its life roughly halved: an overloaded, poorly ventilated motor or one in too hot a room ages very fast.

## Protection rating and duty

- **IP**: two digits, protection against solids (dust) then against water. IP55: protected against dust and water jets.
- **Duty**: S1 continuous at constant load; S3 intermittent, alternating running and rest (e.g. running 40 % of the time).

![Duty types: S1 continuous at constant load, S3 intermittent with 40 % running](/static/content/moteurs/en/m6/service.svg)

## Key points

- T = P / ω, ω = 2π × n / 60.
- IE3: a good choice for a motor that runs for long hours.
- Class F (155 °C); +10 °C beyond halves the life.
