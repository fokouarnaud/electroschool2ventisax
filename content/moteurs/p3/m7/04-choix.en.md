Choosing a contactor means reading its characteristics and comparing them with the need.

![Contactor data: 9 A and 4 kW in AC-3 at 400 V, Ith 25 A in AC-1, 24 V DC coil, one make and one break contact, one million operations](/static/content/moteurs/en/m7/choix.svg)

1. **The category and current**: for a motor, read the current (or power) in **AC-3** at the supply voltage. **Example**: a 4 kW motor at 400 V draws about 7.9 A; a **9 A AC-3** contactor suits. For reversing or inching, choose in AC-4, so larger.
2. **The coil voltage**: that of the control circuit (24 V DC most often); a 230 V AC coil connected to 24 V will never close, a 24 V coil connected to 230 V burns out.
3. **The auxiliary contacts** needed (hold-in, interlock, signalling), built in or added as a block.
4. **The electrical life** (number of operations) compared with the machine's operating rate.

The **thermal current Ith** (in AC-1) is the current the contactor carries continuously on a resistive load; it is not used to choose for a motor.

## Box: NEMA sizes

American documents designate contactors by **NEMA sizes** (00, 0, 1, 2…), each covering a range of powers. The IEC standard reasons by **category and current**. The two only convert approximately:

![Indicative comparison: NEMA size 00 to 9 A, size 0 to 18 A, size 1 to 27 A, size 2 to 45 A in AC-3](/static/content/moteurs/en/m7/nema.svg)

In this course and on IEC equipment, always choose by the **AC-3 current**; the NEMA size only helps read an American document.

## Key points

- Motor: AC-3 current at the supply voltage.
- Coil: the control circuit voltage.
- NEMA only helps read an American document.
